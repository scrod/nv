# Robust iCloud Drive Support

Status: working specification; major design decisions complete

Domain terms follow the project [storage and reconciliation vocabulary](../CONTEXT.md).

## Authority and scope

- iCloud Drive receives tested first-class support. Runtime correctness does not depend on identifying the provider's vendor: NV uses positive provider-management evidence and operation-specific capabilities/results. Dropbox and other File Providers automatically receive compatible behavior but remain best-effort because NV does not validate or promise their complete materialization, version, or status semantics.
- The first-class provider-managed storage contract requires unencrypted `PlainTextFormat` separate-file storage. A database-only, encrypted, RTF, or HTML notes directory must disable encryption when applicable and finish a complete plain-text export before it can operate under this contract.
- The live in-memory store remains authoritative while NV runs. Separate note files are the routine cross-Mac representation of note changes. `Notes & Settings` remains the complete fast-start checkpoint, settings and metadata store, backup artifact, and recovery envelope.
- A noncanonical or conflicting `Notes & Settings` version is recovery evidence, not a routine synchronization input. NV does not merge database branches into the live store during normal startup, background synchronization, or quit.
- The canonical `Notes & Settings` filename and backward-readable keyed archive remain compatible with epoch 4. Epoch 4 need not discover or recover modern uniquely named journals; after a modern crash, modern NV must recover first. NV creates no legacy WAL hard-link, clone, or copied alias.
- NV does not periodically checkpoint `Notes & Settings` or rotate the journal. Before a root becomes editable it has one accepted checkpoint and a fresh journal based on it. After editing stops, closing or changing a still-writable root installs at most one final checkpoint. Every exceptional installation—initial collection creation, startup recovery, rebuild, storage transition, or a **Save Current Collection As…** destination—uses the same checkpoint-installation transaction. The new destination may install one crash-safe bootstrap and one final separate-format checkpoint before it becomes editable; this is not a periodic checkpoint of the active root. Explicit **Recover Notes** imports remain journal-durable and become checkpoint-covered at the next root closure rather than introducing another checkpoint boundary.
- External deletion or replacement of the active notes directory is a storage-authority-independent root-identity failure. NV never retargets the running store from path or content equality and never reconciles the authoritative in-memory store into a replacement directory. It may instead open the replacement as a fresh root or construct a distinct new collection through **Save Current Collection As…** as specified below.
- A possible future RNCryptor-based separate-file format compatible with FSNotes is outside this specification. It will require its own interoperability, key-management, conflict, migration, and recovery contract.

## Product requirements

- A provider-current, materialized `Notes & Settings` opens directly into the complete in-memory store; search and editing then remain synchronous and immediate.
- Provider and network work never runs on the main thread.
- Local work is made journal-durable without waiting for a provider. When NV cannot establish that local durability, it stops admitting new edits instead of treating memory or a successful `write` alone as durable.
- Concurrent external note-file edits are merged when the existing file snapshot supplies a trustworthy base; otherwise every differing version is preserved.
- Network realities appear at startup, while explicitly working offline, on actionable synchronization failure, and while quitting with an unconfirmed checkpoint. Routine local save stages do not appear in the UI.
- File operations retain NV's atomic installation, expected-snapshot conflict detection, transactional reconciliation, path independence, external-move tolerance, and no-`PATH_MAX` goals wherever the filesystem permits them.
- Plain-text separate files remain interoperable with ordinary editors and plain-text iOS applications. Their contents, filenames, and provider metadata are intentionally not protected by NV's database encryption. NV does not require every note file to remain materialized for the process lifetime.

## Normative requirement registry and dependency paths

The following requirement IDs are stable cross-document references. Their prose remains authoritative here even if surrounding sections move. They are a registry, not one total ordering: startup, routine reconciliation, recovery, checkpoint installation, and cleanup use different applicable dependency paths. Timeout, callback arrival, path equality, provider branding, or a successful lower-level operation never substitutes for required evidence.

| Requirement | Claim | Required evidence | What the claim permits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `AUTH-01` | Root authority and continuity | One active root-session incarnation plus positive provider-managed, confirmed ordinary-local, or explicitly unknown authority evidence | Selection of the authority-specific rules below; unknown authority grants no provider or ordinary-local cleanup proof |
| `START-01` | Startup store acceptance | Exact complete checkpoint bytes, successful decode, mandatory journal recovery, and a fresh synchronized journal/claim | Publication of the editable in-memory store |
| `DISC-01` | Discovery scope | Either a complete exact current-known note-candidate snapshot, or the explicitly narrower `CAP-01` current-visible snapshot | Selection of complete-exact or current-visible reconciliation; an incomplete snapshot permits only conservative partial preservation |
| `PUB-01` | Routine note publication | Every selected routine note version represented under matching provider, session, structural, and UUID/LSN fences | Atomic publication of that transaction's permitted result; only complete exact routine publication may add routine resolution eligibility |
| `REC-01` | Database-version recovery publication | One complete exact **Recover Notes** snapshot, every selected database version readable and represented, and both provider fences plus model/session inputs unchanged | Atomic import of deterministic recovery notes and addition of only those database-version identifiers to transient resolution eligibility |
| `DUR-01` | Local checkpoint durability | Settled note-file and journal obligations plus exact checkpoint installation, directory durability, read-back byte equality, decode, checkpoint identity, and mandatory expected-current CAS | A local receipt freezing represented journal and eligible provider-version coverage |
| `QUAL-01` | Provider checkpoint qualification | `DUR-01` plus exact receipt-correlated provider upload qualification when the root is provider-managed; confirmed ordinary-local storage needs only `DUR-01` | Authority-specific cleanup of only the receipt's frozen coverage |
| `CLEAN-01` | Destructive cleanup | Applicable `QUAL-01`, exact coverage membership, ownership where required, and operation-specific removal durability; final journal-claim clearing additionally requires successful root-directory synchronization after the current absence validation | Independent provider-version resolution and journal/claim removal; failure of one member authorizes nothing for another |
| `ROOT-01` | Root-state action gating | The current root-continuity, journal-durability, and lifecycle reasons | The edit, publication, cleanup, and user-action set allowed by the normative matrix below |
| `CKPT-01` | Checkpoint-caller outcomes | One typed result from the canonical checkpoint transaction under the caller's exact expected-current evidence | The caller-specific retry, cancellation, override, retention, or publication behavior in the normative matrix below |
| `WAIT-01` | Waiting and cancellation | Operation ownership plus unchanged correctness evidence; elapsed time is never evidence | Progress and cancellation choices without manufacturing freshness, absence, completion, or failure |
| `CAP-01` | Stable unsupported historical discovery | The exact operation-scoped error and still-active root/session conditions below | Session-scoped current-visible fallback, never current-known completeness or provider-version resolution eligibility |

The principal dependency paths are:

- Startup: establish `AUTH-01`; classify and accept or construct the checkpoint, using `CKPT-01` and `DUR-01` whenever installation occurs; complete mandatory recovery; then satisfy `START-01`.
- Routine exact reconciliation: `AUTH-01` → `DISC-01` → `PUB-01`; later root closure may pass represented eligibility through `DUR-01` → `QUAL-01` → `CLEAN-01`.
- Database **Recover Notes**: `AUTH-01` plus complete exact recovery discovery → `REC-01`; later root closure uses the same `DUR-01` → `QUAL-01` → `CLEAN-01` chain.
- `ROOT-01`, `CKPT-01`, `WAIT-01`, and `CAP-01` are cross-cutting policies, not rungs that every operation traverses.

The provably empty owned-active-journal exception is the only cleanup path that bypasses checkpoint qualification, and it retains its complete local emptiness, ownership, removal, directory-synchronization, and claim-removal preconditions below. Explicit user-confirmed **Discard** is the only provider-version destruction outside `CLEAN-01`.

## Reconciliation evidence terms

- **Provider-version representation** exists only when the selected provider state is the candidate original, an ordinary conflict note, an identity-bound intentional tombstone, or an explicitly recorded input to a successful merge or one-sided local-win decision. Equality of a filename, path, timestamp, or output bytes alone does not establish representation. A complete transaction must represent every selected version and every differing pretransaction live state. This is the implementation contract formalized by `AllSelectedRepresented` and `CompleteFence` in [ReconciliationInterface.tla](tla/ReconciliationInterface.tla) and by `EveryProcessedVersionIsRepresented` in [ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla).
- **Preservation** retains a differing nondeletion note state as a conflict note or retains a deletion as an identity-bound intentional tombstone without publishing the transaction as complete. Preservation from a partial, failed, or canceled transaction does not confer provider-version resolution eligibility and always leaves reconciliation dirty. The complete-publication and partial-preservation paths are separate in [ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla).
- A provider version gains **provider-version resolution eligibility** through exactly two producers: a `PUB-01` complete exact routine reconciliation transaction, or a `REC-01` complete exact database **Recover Notes** run. For either producer, enumeration was complete; every selected version was readable and represented; both provider fences matched; and the root session incarnation plus applicable structural and UUID/LSN inputs were unchanged. Partial or canceled recovery, unclaimed-journal recovery, preservation, **Discard**, and current-visible fallback reconciliation never add eligibility. Automatic resolution additionally requires local durability of its separate-file or intentional-tombstone representation, inclusion in the exact resolution coverage of a read-back-verified checkpoint, and, under provider management, exact upload qualification of that checkpoint. NV need not confirm upload of each separate note file because the uploaded complete checkpoint is the recovery backstop while those files transfer normally. A later complete exact producer may confer eligibility on previously preserved evidence. `ResolutionRequiredHistoricalEligibilityAndDurability` in [ReconciliationSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationSafety.tla), `RecoveryEligibilityRequiredCompleteExactRecovery` and `PartialOrFailedRunsDoNotGainResolutionEligibility` in [RecoveryEvidenceSafety.tla](tla/RecoveryEvidenceSafety.tla) encode these boundaries.
- An **intentional tombstone** records deletion of a stable note UUID or deterministic recovery-note UUID. It is bound to that identity, not to any filename or path, and therefore continues to represent the deletion across local or remote renames. The name-independent representation rule is exercised by `Represents` and the `localRenameRemoteDelete` fixture in [ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla).

## Startup

- On macOS 26 and later, before checking checkpoint freshness, NV reads `NSURLUbiquitousItemIsSyncPausedKey` for the canonical `Notes & Settings`. A paused checkpoint is treated as an interrupted prior transaction: NV resumes synchronization with `NSFileManagerResumeSyncBehaviorPreserveLocalChanges` and then continues the ordinary provider-current startup check. Resume failure presents Retry, choose another notes directory, quit, or—only when the complete checkpoint and every mandatory journal input already satisfy the offline rule below—work offline; the sync controller remains responsible for retrying resume. No custom persisted pause marker is required.
- NV asynchronously coordinates and checks the canonical `Notes & Settings` before loading it.
- If the provider reports that checkpoint as current, it can be decoded, and mandatory legacy/same-host journal discovery finds no unrecovered journal-durable work, NV shows the notes immediately. A retained-journal claim whose covering `checkpointID` equals the accepted checkpoint establishes already-represented local coverage without materializing that journal and does not delay the UI. Unresolved database conflict versions and unclaimed provider-managed journals do not block startup because both are recovery evidence.
- Normal current-checkpoint startup does not wait for a note-directory catalog, note contents, historical-version enumeration, Spotlight, or `NSMetadataQuery`. Routine reconciliation begins in the background after the UI and fresh process journal are ready.
- An explicitly stale or unmaterialized canonical database checkpoint item shows startup progress while NV requests it. `ENOENT` is never itself treated as a new empty database. Checkpoint absence is classified only by this operation-local recipe under the active root session: obtain one complete coordinated shallow root catalog satisfying AV-03; require the canonical name to be absent; query the fixed canonical URL for current, other, unresolved, and asynchronous nonlocal `NSFileVersion` entries; and require every applicable query to complete without a transient, ambiguous, or ordinary error. `NSCocoaErrorDomain`/`NSFeatureUnsupportedError` from the required nonlocal query classifies only history as unavailable under the stable-unsupported rule below; it does not manufacture an exact empty history. Any other failed step leaves checkpoint status unavailable. A second catalog is unnecessary merely to present startup choices: explicit or operation-owned creation later rechecks expected absence inside `NVCheckpointTransaction`, while rebuild and reconciliation retain their own double fences.
- Absence never proves that an existing cloud folder is an empty notes collection. NV may initialize silently only a directory that it created during the current new-collection operation. For any preexisting selected directory with no known checkpoint or versions, the folder chooser must make **Create a New Notes Collection Here** an explicit action; a saved folder that merely appears empty stays in startup recovery. Explicit or operation-owned creation installs and exactly verifies an initial empty checkpoint through the canonical checkpoint transaction before publishing the fresh journal and editable UI. Files or versions arriving after creation are preserved and reconciled as external or recovery evidence rather than silently discarded.
- If updating fails or the user cancels materialization before acceptance, NV publishes no store and offers retry, quit, choose another notes directory, or—only when complete local checkpoint bytes and every mandatory recovery journal can be materialized, validated, and recovered—work offline. Mandatory inputs are the legacy exact-name journal, the one unambiguous active journal claimed by this Mac, and any same-host retained journal whose recorded covering checkpoint is not the accepted checkpoint; unclaimed provider-managed journals do not block startup. Canceling an app-owned progress operation asks NV to stop its own coordination/materialization work and return to these choices; provider cancellation or elapsed time never reclassifies the item. There is no read-only mode.
- Working offline loads those locally complete bytes and recovered journal state, permits edits, and reports offline status until either current-known note-file reconciliation succeeds or `CAP-01` permits current-visible fallback reconciliation to settle every visible obligation. The latter changes the status from offline to a session-continuous nonblocking limited-history indication rather than claiming current-known completeness. An unavailable or invalid mandatory journal cannot be bypassed because it may contain journal-durable work newer than the checkpoint.
- A legacy exact-name journal is recovered first. Only after its result is installed does NV classify and recover this Mac's modern active journal and any retained journal lacking exact accepted-checkpoint coverage. In the confirmed ordinary-local backup-restoration case defined below, exactly one unambiguous journal-recovery-eligible unclaimed journal is recovered automatically before NV publishes a fresh journal or editable UI.
- If no acceptable canonical database checkpoint can produce a startup store, startup offers **Rebuild from Note Files** for any NV-readable separate-file root whose applicable stable snapshot can be established. An exact-capable provider-managed root uses one complete current-known note-catalog/version snapshot; confirmed ordinary-local storage uses its complete coordinated current note catalog; and `CAP-01` may select the same stable current-visible double-catalog snapshot as fallback reconciliation. Transient, ambiguous, unavailable, or incomplete discovery never selects the fallback and cannot publish a rebuild.
- Rebuild classifies direct-child regular files before selecting input. It excludes hidden names and the exact NV artifact set below. Case-insensitive built-in mappings are plain text (`txt`, `text`, `utf8`, `taskpaper`), RTF (`rtf`), and HTML (`html`, `htm`); NV performs no content sniffing. Every other extension group requires an operation-local user decision assigning one of those existing decoders or explicitly ignoring the group. Ignored files remain untouched and outside the selected set; an unresolved group, an extension assigned to more than one decoder, or a selected file that fails its assigned decoder blocks publication. This one-run classification recovers custom extensions without inventing persisted format state. Each selected current or historical version uses the decoder assigned to its candidate URL's extension.
- NV materializes the selected snapshot with progress and decodes through the existing per-format note initializer rather than `NotationPrefs`' `SingleDatabaseFormat` default. Safe defaults initialize only settings absent from note files and never choose the reconstructed storage contract. A provider-managed rebuild targets unencrypted `PlainTextFormat` and completes the existing detached all-note write/conversion path before the replacement checkpoint can publish. A confirmed ordinary-local snapshot whose selected files all use one decoder retains that format. Mixed ordinary-local input requires an explicit target separate-file format and complete detached conversion; NV never silently chooses a lossy target. Choosing plain text for RTF or HTML input requires the same formatting-loss disclosure as provider conversion.
- The detached replacement imports every distinct readable selected state without inferring deletions and repeats the applicable fence if the directory changes. It publishes no partial store: cancel, crash, an unreadable selected state, a failed export or fence, or any other failure leaves the original artifacts untouched and requires a later retry. After a stable rebuild, NV atomically installs and verifies one complete checkpoint using expected absence or the exact classified predecessor, creates and publishes a fresh journal, and only then presents the normal editable UI. A current-visible rebuild enters that UI with a session-continuous nonblocking limited-history status and gains no provider-version resolution eligibility. Rejected database versions remain recovery evidence for ordinary **Recover Notes**. Spotlight results cannot establish rebuild completeness. `ExactRebuildRequiresCompleteEnumeration`, `CurrentVisibleRebuildRequiresStableUnsupportedAndStatus`, `EveryRebuildInstallMatchedExpectedCheckpoint`, and `AbsentCheckpointRebuildUsedExpectedAbsence` in [StartupSafety.tla](tla/StartupSafety.tla) formalize the publication scopes and checkpoint precondition; file selection, decoding, and conversion remain implementation/crash-test obligations outside that state abstraction.

## Routine separate-file reconciliation

- A directory catalog reads names and cheap identity/revision metadata without requesting every note's contents. Only new, changed, and unmatched rename candidates require content.
- A coordinated shallow catalog is partitioned before it participates in any transaction. The authoritative **note candidate** set is the union of current note-file direct children, note identities and names retained by the live model and last accepted note catalog, and note URLs supplied by presenter move/version callbacks. Visible files shaped `Title (conflict recovery <UUID>).ext` are ordinary note candidates: existing atomic replacement creates them specifically so routine reconciliation will preserve/import their contents. The separate **NV artifact candidate** set is exactly the canonical checkpoint, the legacy exact-name journal, modern `Interim Note-Changes.<journalID>` journals, `.nv-root.lock`, and hidden operation residue matching `.nv-write-<UUID>`. No broader title or “recovery” pattern is reserved. Both partitions reuse the same catalog enumeration and provider lane, but they have independent projections, dirty generations, completion, and retry state. Artifact status/recovery work may not enter the routine note decision matrix, fail its note-catalog fence, retain its note dirty generation, or delay its publication; note work likewise may not clear an artifact obligation.
- A current-known note transaction begins with a successful complete coordinated shallow note-catalog projection. For every authoritative note candidate URL—not merely catalog-changed, unmatched, callback-dirty, or content-selected URLs—NV combines its current version, locally known other and unresolved versions, and asynchronously reported nonlocal versions, deduplicating them by persistent version identifier. This all-candidate metadata enumeration is required because a provider version set may change without changing the shallow catalog or delivering a callback first. Enumeration does not read note content; only new, changed, unmatched, or newly disclosed selected versions are prepared and materialized. `NSMetadataQuery` and Spotlight results are never authoritative inputs and cannot gate either partition, establish completeness, or clear dirty.
- Current-known note completeness covers every note candidate URL disclosed through those sources and every version returned for it by the required `NSFileVersion` calls. It does not claim that Apple has disclosed a historical or remote item absent from the current note catalog, retained note identity, and presenter callbacks. Fixed artifact names provide the corresponding recovery/status discovery ceiling in their separate transactions; they are not part of the routine note completeness claim. If the platform cannot satisfy the applicable candidate/version-discovery contract, that partition's enumeration is incomplete and its complete publication or recovery action remains unavailable.
- A transient, ambiguous, or ordinary failure to enumerate nonlocal versions makes the snapshot incomplete. NV may conservatively process known readable states, but it neither resolves or deletes versions nor reports reconciliation complete; failed and newly arriving versions remain candidates for a later transaction. Only `CAP-01` selects the current-visible fallback below for the current root session.
- NV prepares selected URLs with Apple's batch coordination API. Each nested operation uses the accessor URL supplied by `NSFileCoordinator`; nonlocal version contents are accessed only through a coordinated read of the version URL, so provider preparation and materialization occur only for the selected set.
- Detached reconciliation records the model's structural generation plus affected notes' UUID/LSN values. Structural generation changes when a note is added or removed, a rename changes filesystem naming, or storage format or serialization policy changes. A verified move of the same open root under unchanged provider authority changes neither note-to-file structure nor structural generation: rooted descriptors, operation-local file references, and the provider APIs validated by AV-08 remain attached to the same items, so detached work remains eligible under its existing provider, session, structural, and UUID/LSN fences. Root replacement cancels the old incarnation and makes any reopened root fresh. A provider authority/account change instead fences the old incarnation and invalidates its captured work; its successor becomes active only through the provisional handoff below. Neither case needs a redundant structural-generation change. UUID/LSN validation separately fences note content, title, and labels.
- A second provider catalog token and exact set of persistent version identifiers form the exact provider fence. The token and identifier set are independent opaque equality values: equality of either does not imply equality of the other, and NV infers no ordering, increment, or freshness from their values. A mismatch in either invalidates complete publication. Main-thread commit additionally requires unchanged model inputs and the same active root session incarnation; it commits the entire candidate transaction and performs no filesystem access. `ExactProviderFence` in [ReconciliationInterface.tla](tla/ReconciliationInterface.tla) and the actions that independently change only the provider catalog token or only the persistent version identifier set in [ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla) formalize these obligations.
- A partial snapshot may publish only additive represented conflict notes and their decision provenance while the same root session incarnation, structural generation, and affected UUID/LSN inputs remain current. It may retain an already-existing tombstone but may not create or apply a deletion, replace the original note or name, resolve or delete provider versions, or report reconciliation complete, and it always retains the dirty follow-up obligation. Otherwise NV discards the whole detached candidate. See `CommitPartialPreservation` and `EveryPartialCommitWasConservative` in [ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla).
- Current-visible fallback reconciliation reuses NV's coordinated double-catalog/current-file compare-and-swap transaction. It requires a complete shallow namespace catalog, readable selected current states, equal first and second current-visible catalogs/snapshots, unchanged root/model fences, and every through-generation callback to be accounted for by current evidence or an explicitly delivered immutable version. It may apply the ordinary current-file merge, rename, and deletion decisions and consume that operational dirty generation, but it never claims current-known completeness, confers provider-version resolution eligibility, resolves provider versions, or proves that undisclosed history does not exist. A failed current catalog/read/fence remains dirty; a stable unsupported result merely suppresses retries whose sole purpose would be rediscovering the missing facility. A fresh root session attempts exact discovery again; NV persists no capability profile.
- The provider lane records note notifications in one note dirty-generation high-water mark and artifact notifications in an independent artifact status/recovery generation. A root callback may conservatively increment both; exact-name classification may later show that only one partition changed, but neither generation may clear the other. Note notifications received after a note transaction starts establish a newer note obligation and cause one coalesced follow-up transaction; the active transaction may not clear that obligation, even if an immediate note-catalog projection still appears unchanged. A generic external-note-change callback also retains a coalesced pre-callback note catalog/version projection for its affected names; an exact note transaction may discharge it only after a stable exact observation differs from that projection, while a version callback requires its persistent identifier in the exact note-version set. A stable current-visible fallback may instead discharge only the note obligations its stable current evidence or explicitly delivered immutable version accounted for. Failed fencing, partial preservation, discard, cancellation, unreadability, incomplete enumeration without the stable-unsupported fallback, and an unwitnessed callback always retain the applicable partition dirty with bounded-backoff retry. Exact-path state history remains governed by `EveryCompleteCommitSetCorrectDirtyState`, `EveryRetryPathKeptDirty`, and `LateProviderChangeKeepsDirty` in [ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla); `EveryFallbackCommitHadStableCurrentFences`, `FallbackRequiresStableUnsupportedResult`, `FallbackNeverChangesResolutionEligibility`, and `FallbackConsumedOnlyItsServicedGeneration` in [CurrentVisibleFallbackSafety.tla](tla/CurrentVisibleFallbackSafety.tla) refine the narrower scope without multiplying the exact model's arbitrary transaction history. Callback-before-catalog-visibility and the note/artifact projection split remain explicit implementation refinements rather than reasons to weaken either dirty invariant.
- User edits update the live store immediately, enqueue journal durability, and coalesce each note's separate-file write obligation to its latest generation. A provider completion never overwrites a newer in-memory generation.
- Existing expected-byte compare-and-swap remains the note-write conflict detector. A concurrent external and local content change uses the last trustworthy separate-file bytes as its merge base; `NVMergedText` handles plain text. A successful reconciliation or note-file write supplies the exact accepted snapshot. Before either occurs in a newly accepted session, the exact accepted checkpoint may seed the base for a note only because the checkpoint-installation contract settled that note's separate-file obligation before serializing the represented state. A later exact snapshot replaces that seed. If neither source is trustworthy or the merge is unsafe, NV preserves the alternate as an ordinary conflict note with a fresh UUID. When external content still equals the base and local content wins unchanged, NV nevertheless records both the live snapshot and external version as decision ancestry before that version can gain provider-version resolution eligibility; `LocalWinsRecordsBaseAncestry` in [ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla) prevents output equality from erasing that evidence.
- Plain-text files carry no NV UUID field or extended attribute. Identity-first matching means stable local filesystem identity when available, followed by the existing unambiguous filename and content matching. If a version cannot be associated with an existing UUID, NV treats its readable state as a new ordinary note instead of inventing a shared file identity.
- For a provider-managed root, the selected candidate set includes every known unresolved `NSFileVersion` of a changed note item, not only its canonical version. Eligibility remains transient in the root session until a root-closing checkpoint receipt freezes the exact eligible identifiers still represented by its frozen store. After that receipt reaches the durability chain above, NV resolves each covered version independently through `NSFileVersion`; a per-version failure preserves that version for rediscovery and does not invalidate the checkpoint or block root closure. A conflict note or tombstone written by a partial or current-visible fallback transaction is preservation evidence, not permission to resolve that version.
- Deletion of an externally unchanged note is selected transactionally. Deletion concurrent with changed content or a rename preserves an identity-bound tombstone for the original note and the changed or independently renamed state as a new conflict note. A rename cannot evade or invalidate an existing tombstone.
- Renames coordinate source and destination together and remain serialized with note writes. Paths and timestamps alone never establish identity.
- Changes to `Notes & Settings` or another NV artifact while NV runs increment only the artifact status/recovery obligation after exact-name classification. They do not mutate the live store, enter the note-catalog projection or note persistent-version set, or delay routine note reconciliation.

The following decision matrix is normative after identity association succeeds. When stable identity is absent or the permitted fallback is unmatched or ambiguous, the final row governs before any rename/edit row is considered. Fixture names correspond to `Scenarios`, `ExpectedCanonicalOutcome`, and `ExpectedCanonicalArtifact` in [ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationDecisionSafety.tla). Iterating canonical, unresolved, and nonlocal versions in a different order must not change the final candidate, conflict artifacts, tombstones, or recorded ancestry. Exact duplicate states are deduplicated; every differing unresolved or nonlocal state is preserved rather than selected as the original solely because of arrival order.

| Conflict condition | Model fixture | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remote content changed; local content and name remain at the base | `fastForward`, `filenameContentIdentity` | Select the canonical remote content. Filename/content fallback is valid only when both identify one unambiguous note. |
| Local content changed; remote remains at the base | `localEditRemoteUnchanged` | Keep local content and record both local and remote/base ancestry. |
| Both sides changed content with a trustworthy, safe merge base | `twoEditsMerge` | Publish the merged content and record both inputs. |
| Both sides changed content without a base or without a safe merge | `missingBase`, `unsafeMerge` | Keep the local original and preserve the remote state as an ordinary conflict note. |
| Only the remote side renamed | `remoteRenameOnly` | Select the canonical remote name without changing content. |
| Only the local side renamed | `localRenameRemoteUnchanged` | Keep the local name and record both inputs as ancestry. |
| Local rename plus remote content edit, or remote rename plus local content edit | `renameRemoteEdit`, `remoteRenameLocalEdit` | Combine the changed name with the independently changed content and record both inputs. |
| One side renamed while both sides made safely mergeable content edits | `localRenameTwoEditsMerge`, `remoteRenameTwoEditsMerge` | Publish merged content under the independently changed name and record all inputs. |
| Both sides selected different non-base names, with or without a remote content edit | `renameOnlyRace`, `renameRace` | Keep the local candidate and preserve the remote named state as a conflict note; timestamps and arrival order do not choose a winner. |
| Remote deletion races a local edit or rename | `deleteEdit`, `localRenameRemoteDelete` | Tombstone the original identity and preserve the local changed or renamed state as a conflict note. |
| Local deletion races a remote edit or rename | `localDeleteRemoteEdit`, `localDeleteRemoteRename` | Keep the original identity tombstoned and preserve the remote changed or renamed state as a conflict note. |
| Canonical deletion coexists with differing unresolved and nonlocal versions | `multiVersion` | Select deletion of the original identity and preserve every differing additional version. |
| Stable identity is absent, fallback is ambiguous, or only path/timestamp agrees | `unmatchedIdentity`, `ambiguousIdentity`, `pathTimestampIdentity` | Do not attach the version to the existing UUID; preserve it as a new ordinary conflict note with a fresh UUID. |

## Database recovery evidence

- NV catalogs the canonical and unresolved `NSFileVersion` entries for `Notes & Settings` without routinely materializing or decoding noncanonical versions.
- Recovery versions remain discoverable in the existing status UI. Their existence does not produce a modal alert or change the live store. The only recovery actions are **Recover Notes** and explicit **Discard**; NV offers no per-version picker, neither exports nor installs a recovery checkpoint, and does not restore its settings or metadata. The accepted database checkpoint is the sole source of database-scoped settings and metadata; host preferences, Keychain values, and current-host journal claims remain host-local. Preserving concurrent changes to those fields is intentionally unsupported.
- **Recover Notes** takes one coordinated snapshot of the persistent version identifiers for every local and nonlocal recovery version known in that snapshot. It records the provider catalog token and repeats the exact provider fence before declaring the run complete. Completion requires complete enumeration, no failed member, the same exact identifier set and provider catalog token, and representation of every snapshot member; `CompleteRunsHadExactAllVersionSnapshots` in [RecoveryEvidenceSafety.tla](tla/RecoveryEvidenceSafety.tla) captures that boundary. NV reads each version as an immutable snapshot, continues past a version that cannot be materialized or decoded, and reports partial completion while preserving failed and newly arrived versions for a later run. An incomplete nonlocal enumeration permits preservation of readable states but confers no new provider-version resolution eligibility and forbids resolving any candidate or declaring recovery complete. Identical note records and duplicate recovered states across versions are ignored; a missing or differing note state is imported only as a new conflict note, never over an existing note and never as evidence of deletion. This deliberately conservative recovery requires no checkpoint ancestry, retained database merge base, field-level database merge matrix, database tombstone protocol, or whole-checkpoint recovery transaction.
- Each imported recovery note receives an ordinary deterministic UUID in one fixed UUIDv5 recovery namespace. Its name input is the archived source-note UUID followed by a SHA-256 digest of canonical recovery state encoding `v1`: a domain tag; NFC-normalized, length-prefixed UTF-8 title and plaintext body; and the count plus lexicographically sorted NFC-normalized, length-prefixed UTF-8 labels. Dates, selection, filenames, formatting attributes, LSNs, sync state, and filesystem or provider metadata are excluded. Repeating recovery of the same user-authored state therefore finds the same live note or tombstone and does not import it again; a genuinely different recovered state receives a different identity. No recovery UUID field or ledger is added. Existing note UUIDs, including every UUID read from an epoch-4 checkpoint, remain unchanged.
- NV resolves or deletes a recovery version only after the user explicitly invokes recovery or discard. Successful processing alone does not authorize automatic resolution: a completed **Recover Notes** run contributes its persistent identifiers to the same transient resolution-eligibility map used by routine exact reconciliation, and the next root-closing checkpoint receipt may freeze only identifiers whose separate-file or intentional-tombstone representations are locally durable and present in its frozen store. Under provider management, exact upload qualification must cover that same checkpoint identity and resolution coverage; a stale, mismatched, or unrelated callback is no evidence. Each covered version is then resolved independently, while a failure or newly arrived version remains a recovery candidate and does not block root closure. Explicit **Discard** remains the only direct destructive path outside this checkpoint-coverage rule. `RecoveryEligibilityRequiredCompleteExactRecovery` in [RecoveryEvidenceSafety.tla](tla/RecoveryEvidenceSafety.tla) proves what may enter the shared map; `ResolutionRequiredHistoricalEligibilityAndDurability` in [ReconciliationSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationSafety.tla) proves the generic later durability chain.
- **Discard** confirms once, captures a coordinated snapshot of persistent version identifiers, and resolves and removes each selected recovery version independently. A version that fails, disappears, changes identity, or arrives after the snapshot remains visible; discard never broadens itself to versions the user did not confirm.
- A recovery version that cannot be decoded remains preserved. No version identity, timestamp, local write order, or arrival order authorizes overwriting live data.

## Journal identity and recovery

The journal-retention protocol tracks only the following state:

| State | Location | Contents and authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journal artifact | Shared notes directory | `Interim Note-Changes.<journalID>` with its integrity-checked ID, exact checkpoint base identity, and records. The artifact alone grants no append or deletion authority. |
| Active claim | Current application/current user/`kCFPreferencesCurrentHost` | `NVActiveJournal.<journalID>` containing the rooted-directory bookmark. After synchronized publication it grants this process append authority while the directory lock is held. |
| Retained claim | Same current-host preference domain | `NVRetainedJournal.<journalID>` containing a schema version, rooted-directory bookmark, and exact locally verified covering `checkpointID`. It grants same-host recovery and cleanup authority, but never append authority or upload evidence. |
| Represented-journal set | Current `NVRootSession` memory | Exact active and retained journal IDs represented by the accepted live state. It is reconstructed from claims and recovery; losing it in a crash cannot authorize cleanup. |
| Checkpoint coverage | Immutable `NVCheckpointReceipt` memory | Exact represented-journal set frozen into one locally installed checkpoint attempt, alongside that checkpoint's identity and local version. It is the sole journal set an upload qualification may retire. |

For one journal ID, the legal ownership states are `active`, `demoting`, `retained`, `deleting`, and `absent`:

- `active` is a journal plus its synchronized active claim.
- `demoting` is a journal plus its synchronized retained claim and possibly the old active claim.
- `retained` is a journal plus its synchronized retained claim.
- `deleting` means namespace removal has begun or completed while an active or retained claim remains.
- `absent` means neither artifact nor claim remains.

`deleting` has two conservative crash-recovery subphases. In `pending-durability`, removal succeeded or was ambiguous but the containing directory has not subsequently synchronized. In `durable`, `-[NVRootedDirectory synchronize:]` succeeded after the most recent operation-local absence validation. Claim removal is forbidden before `durable`; a restart does not infer durability from absence alone. NV never removes an active claim before the retained claim is synchronized, and never removes the final active or retained claim during cleanup before journal removal is directory-durable. Provider observations and upload qualification are transaction-local and are never persisted in either claim.

- Every modern journal creation attempt generates a fresh random UUID `journalID`. Its path is `Interim Note-Changes.<journalID>` and its integrity-checked header contains the same `journalID` and the base identity of the exact checkpoint bytes it extends. A modern checkpoint contributes its random `checkpointID`; a loaded epoch-4 checkpoint contributes format tag `0x02` plus the SHA-256 digest computed from its exact archived bytes, without modifying those bytes or creating an intermediate checkpoint.
- NV exclusively creates that path and writes and synchronizes the header. It then writes a top-level preference named `NVActiveJournal.<journalID>` in the exact current-application/current-user/`kCFPreferencesCurrentHost` domain. Its value is the opened `NVRootedDirectory`'s bookmark. NV synchronizes that preference and only then accepts edits. A failed attempt never reuses its ID.
- The current-host journal registry uses direct per-journal entries, not one global `activeJournalID` and not one read-modify-written dictionary. A second prefix, `NVRetainedJournal.<journalID>`, records a closed same-host journal that remains recovery evidence because its covering checkpoint lacks applicable upload qualification. Its property-list value contains only a schema version, the rooted-directory bookmark, and the exact locally verified covering `checkpointID`. It contains no writer ID, provider profile, upload flag, Bloom filter, or historical journal set. A retained claim is not append authority and is not evidence that its checkpoint uploaded.
- A temporary launch records its own active and retained entries without changing `GlobalPrefs`' saved default-directory bookmark, so simultaneous NV instances using different folders have independent records. Bookmark bytes are opaque and may change when refreshed; no bookmark is compared or used as a key directly.
- After opening and locking the requested rooted directory, startup enumerates both prefixes in the exact current-host domain. It resolves each bookmark against the open directory descriptor and validates each keyed `journalID` against its journal header. Exactly one valid active candidate identifies the interrupted append session this Mac may recover automatically; any number of valid retained candidates may match because none is appendable.
- Multiple different matching active IDs authorize no append, deletion, or automatic replay. **Recover Interrupted Changes** snapshots every matching candidate under the continuously held directory lock, requires every artifact and valid prefix to be materialized and classifiable, applies the multi-journal recovery rules, and installs one exactly verified replacement checkpoint representing the result.
- Before cleanup or demotion begins, cancellation, an unavailable or invalid candidate, or failed or ambiguous checkpoint installation leaves every candidate artifact and active claim unchanged. After checkpoint verification, NV independently cleans each covered journal under the applicable qualification or demotes it to retained ownership. Cancellation becomes unavailable when the first irreversible member transition begins.
- A completed member stays completed. A failed or unprocessed member remains conservatively `active`, `demoting`, `retained`, or `deleting`. A `deleting` member retries only its remaining directory-durability, claim-removal, and preference-synchronization tail and needs no new checkpoint qualification while absence remains validated. If the artifact is present again, NV preserves it under its claim and requires ordinary recovery and qualification before another removal.
- NV creates a fresh active journal only after every former active ID is absent or durably retained. Partial member failure therefore cannot reopen editing beside another active claim.
- A retained candidate whose covering `checkpointID` equals the accepted checkpoint is already locally represented without reading its content. If the identities differ, the retained journal becomes a mandatory same-host recovery input: NV materializes and classifies its valid prefix before editing, and only an exactly verified replacement checkpoint that represents the result may become its new covering checkpoint. Retained claims never enter the unclaimed-journal recovery UI.
- If a matching bookmark resolves as stale, NV replaces only that entry's bookmark with the current `NVRootedDirectory` bookmark and synchronizes it. It never refreshes an unmatched entry because this process neither opened nor locked that folder. An unresolvable entry is preserved because its folder may be offline or disconnected.
- A matching active or retained claim whose artifact appears absent enters or resumes `deleting/pending-durability`; a coordinated current catalog alone never clears it. While the directory lock remains held, NV revalidates absence in that operation and calls `-[NVRootedDirectory synchronize:]`. Only successful directory synchronization advances to `deleting/durable`, after which NV may remove and synchronize the current-host claim. Synchronization failure preserves the claim and retries later; a present or reappearing artifact returns to claimed recovery handling.
- The fixed writer-independent `.nv-root.lock` file supplies the root-session-lifetime advisory lock shared by modern NV processes on this Mac. NV acquires it before writable startup and holds it continuously until that root session has closed, drained provider work, and settled journal ownership; folder switching then releases the old root's lock before opening the new root. NV refuses writable modern operation when the lock cannot be obtained or the filesystem does not support it. A modern journal inode has no second lock because every compliant local discovery, claim, append, and cleanup already requires the directory lock; inode locking remains only for legacy exact-name journal recovery. Neither lock excludes NV on another Mac.
- Journal recovery validates the header and records sequentially. Every complete integrity-valid record before the first invalid or torn record forms the valid prefix; a torn final record is treated as an interrupted append, while corruption stops replay at that point. NV never truncates or repairs the discovered artifact in place and preserves it until recovered state reaches the required replacement-checkpoint durability.
- Journal recovery eligibility has three cases. A journal whose base identity exactly matches the accepted database checkpoint replays its valid prefix normally. A journal whose valid records are all already represented by the accepted database checkpoint becomes eligible for cleanup after the checkpoint satisfies the applicable ordinary-local or provider-managed durability rule. A base-mismatched journal may not overwrite an existing note, apply a deletion, or restore settings or metadata; recovery may only preserve differing non-deletion note states as deterministic conflict notes. LSN ordering proves order only inside one journal. When several journals require recovery, NV preserves each journal's internal record order while permitting arbitrary traversal between journals; an identical already-represented state is idempotent, a later same-journal record may supersede an earlier one, no cross-journal order is inferred, and an applied deletion remains terminal unless a later record in that same journal updates the note. Every differing nondeletion note state must remain canonical, conflict-preserved, idempotently represented, same-journal-superseded, or covered by the accepted replacement checkpoint; no traversal order may discard it. [MultiJournalRecoverySafety.tla](tla/MultiJournalRecoverySafety.tla) checks that obligation for three records across two journals and two notes, including within-journal order, update/update, delete/update, duplicate, independent-note, and base-mismatch cases.
- NV never resumes appending to a discovered journal. Startup first recovers a journal-recovery-eligible active journal into a verified replacement checkpoint. If that checkpoint satisfies the root's applicable ordinary-local or provider-managed durability rule, NV removes the old journal, clears its active record, and creates a fresh journal.
- If an exactly verified local checkpoint does not satisfy the root's cleanup rule—because provider-managed upload qualification is unavailable, pending, ambiguous, overridden, or failed, or because storage authority remains unknown—NV retains same-host ownership instead of making the closed journal unclaimed. While holding the directory lock and after append has permanently stopped, it writes and synchronizes `NVRetainedJournal.<journalID>` with that exact covering checkpoint identity, removes and synchronizes `NVActiveJournal.<journalID>`, and only then may terminate or create and publish a fresh active journal. A crash before retained-record synchronization leaves the active record authoritative; a crash after retained synchronization but before active-record removal may leave both roles for the same ID. On restart that same-ID dual role is an interrupted transition, not two writers: exact covering-checkpoint validation either completes the demotion or recovers through the active role. Different active IDs remain ambiguous.
- When NV starts from a retained claim whose covering checkpoint exactly equals the accepted checkpoint, it adds that journal ID to the root session's represented-journal set. Ordinary live-store edits, reconciliations, settings changes, and intentional tombstones preserve that represented status only within the session. A later full checkpoint receipt captures the exact active and retained journal IDs represented by its frozen store. After exact local installation, NV may advance each unqualified retained claim in that set to the new covering `checkpointID`. A crash before an advance preserves the older claim as conservative evidence, but because checkpoint identities have equality semantics only, its mismatch with the accepted checkpoint takes the mandatory materialize/classify/recheckpoint path before editing; NV infers no ancestry that would permit a fast path.
- One applicable upload qualification for a full checkpoint authorizes cleanup of every same-host active or retained journal in that receipt's coverage set. Cleanup remains independent per journal; one success never authorizes a journal absent from the receipt.
- Each cleanup coordinates removal, synchronizes the root directory, then removes and synchronizes the current-host claim. A removal failure that positively revalidates the original artifact leaves the prior state. Successful or ambiguous removal enters `deleting/pending-durability` with the claim intact. One successful root-directory synchronization advances every already validated pending removal in that directory to `deleting/durable`; failure leaves all of them pending. Their later claim tails remain independent.
- Once removal is directory-durable, claim-removal or preference-synchronization failure leaves `deleting/durable` and retries only the claim tail. Termination preserves the claim. On restart, coordinated absence validation must be followed by successful `-[NVRootedDirectory synchronize:]` before claim removal; absence alone is insufficient. A present or reappearing artifact remains claimed recovery evidence and requires ordinary recovery and qualification before another removal.
- Independently, NV may remove an owned active journal without checkpoint upload qualification when it is **provably empty**. Append must have stopped permanently; the WAL queue must have no pending or failed record; the artifact must have the exact valid header and zero record bytes; and the directory lock plus matching active claim must establish same-host ownership. Removal and directory synchronization precede claim removal. Any trailing, torn, malformed, unreadable, or otherwise uncertain bytes make the journal nonempty recovery evidence.
- A retained backlog alone requires the next orderly root closure to install one fresh-ID full checkpoint even when no note or setting changed. This is a closure-time cleanup retry, not periodic checkpointing. On macOS 11.5 through 15 it creates a fresh post-install upload transition that may qualify and retire the complete covered backlog; on macOS 26 and later the exact version-returning upload may do the same. No provider or network fairness is assumed, so a backlog remains while every later qualification attempt fails.
- If termination was interrupted after checkpoint installation, recovery compares the valid journal records with the accepted database checkpoint under that journal recovery eligibility matrix and treats already represented UUID/LSN states as idempotent. Base identities and LSN values establish no order across different journals.
- A backup restored into a confirmed ordinary local directory does not need the original host preference. If exactly one structurally valid unclaimed journal is journal-recovery-eligible against the accepted restored checkpoint, NV recovers it automatically while holding the directory lock and does not ask the user to choose. Zero candidates require no recovery. Multiple eligible candidates authorize no automatic replay or deletion, but startup offers explicit **Recover Notes from Journals** over one exact directory-locked snapshot. The action requires every selected journal to be readable and structurally classifiable, applies the same within-journal-order/arbitrary-cross-journal traversal rules, and installs one exactly verified replacement checkpoint before editing. Because confirmed ordinary-local authority plus the directory lock excludes both cloud and local writers, successful local checkpoint durability may then remove each completely processed selected journal independently; a failed member remains preserved and discoverable. An invalid, unavailable, or changed selected member prevents complete recovery and cleanup rather than silently starting around possible durable work.
- An unclaimed journal under provider management may still be active on another Mac. NV catalogs its current and all known local and nonlocal file versions as recovery evidence but does not routinely materialize, replay, append to, resolve, or delete them. Explicit **Recover Notes** snapshots their persistent version identifiers, reads each valid prefix, and imports otherwise missing or differing non-deletion states with the same deterministic recovery-note identity. It never applies deletions or settings from an unclaimed journal and never resolves or deletes a provider-managed journal version that may still have a remote writer.
- Under provider management, only a process whose matching current-host active or retained claim proves same-host ownership may remove a modern journal automatically. A nonempty journal additionally requires the applicable covering-checkpoint durability above; the independently provably empty owned-active-journal branch requires only its exact local emptiness and removal-durability proof. Confirmed ordinary-local backup restoration is the sole unclaimed cleanup exception: exactly one eligible journal may recover automatically, while an explicit complete multi-journal recovery may process its exact selected set, because the directory lock excludes every local writer and no cloud writer exists. During ordinary cleanup the record is cleared only after journal removal is durable, so a crash leaves a conservative stale pointer rather than undiscoverable work.

## Execution and file-provider coordination

- A serial durability lane owns ordinary append and synchronization through the materialized journal descriptor held open for the active root-session lifetime. It does not wait for provider work. A journal obligation remains pending until every byte in its outbox has been written and `NVBarrierSyncFileDescriptor` succeeds; `write` completion alone never reports durability. [JournalLaneIndependence.tla](tla/JournalLaneIndependence.tla) checks that every admitted edit reaches those local durability stages even when provider work remains permanently stalled, under fairness only for NV's append and barrier-sync actions.
- A separate serial provider lane owns coordinated catalogs, selected content reads, note-file writes and renames, checkpoint installation, version inspection, and journal namespace operations: exclusive creation and header synchronization, discovery, demotion, or cleanup. Only after activation has durably created the header and current-host claim does the provider lane hand the open descriptor to the durability lane for ordinary append and `fsync`. Serial order preserves NV's logical filesystem order without blocking the UI.
- Each opened root has one root session that owns its provider lane, presenter, vnode/FSEvents observation, root-identity state, cancellation state, and independent note/artifact dirty generations. Every activation or reopening creates a fresh, never-reused incarnation, even for the same bookmark, descriptor, path, catalog token, or visible contents. A verified same-directory move under unchanged provider authority retains it.
- A confirmed provider authority or account change first authority-fences the old incarnation: old callbacks may no longer publish provider-derived state or authorize cleanup, while a healthy existing WAL continues to durably accept edits. NV creates a **provisional successor**, transfers the continuously held rooted descriptor, directory lock, active journal and claim, and pending local obligations without closing or duplicating them, drains the old observers, and attaches successor-owned observation.
- The provisional successor is the local durability owner but is not yet the active provider-authority incarnation. Provider publication remains frozen while it revalidates rooted namespace identity and journal recoverability. Successful revalidation atomically publishes that never-reused successor as active. Ambiguity leaves it provisional and retryable; it never makes either old callbacks or provisional provider results publishable. This handoff adds no persistent state, journal, or scheduler.
- Confirmed namespace deletion or replacement cancels the old or provisional incarnation; opening the replacement or a newly saved collection creates a fresh one. Every asynchronous operation may publish filesystem, model, or UI results only while its captured incarnation is the exact active, uncancelled incarnation. Pointer-address reuse or equality of visible inputs cannot establish session equality. `ReplaceRootSessionIncarnationWithSameInputs` and `RootSessionIncarnationsAreNeverReused` in [ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationFencingSafety.tla) model the atomic promotion boundary; the nonpublishing provisional interval refines to stuttering. A numeric global completion generation is unnecessary.
- NV registers one `NSFilePresenter` for the notes directory and attaches one `DISPATCH_SOURCE_TYPE_VNODE` source to the already open rooted-directory descriptor for `DISPATCH_VNODE_DELETE`, `DISPATCH_VNODE_RENAME`, and `DISPATCH_VNODE_REVOKE`. Both target the private serial provider queue. Rename enters ordinary root-move verification; revoke means availability/identity is inconclusive; a delete event is positive evidence that the opened vnode was unlinked and starts post-delete identity classification. FSEvents remains a coarse invalidation fallback for ordinary editors and filesystems that do not supply the vnode event. Presenter callbacks enqueue provider work only through their captured root session.
- `NSFileCoordinator`, `NSFilePresenter`, and the advisory lock coordinate this Mac and its local provider extension only; none is a cross-Mac lock. Remote convergence comes from provider-delivered versions plus NV's compare-and-swap, merge, and conservative preservation rules.
- Coordinator URLs are transient and mandatory inside accessors. `NVRootedDirectory`'s open directory descriptor, bookmark, and file-reference identity remain authoritative between operations.
- A **path-only root move** is a Finder- or provider-driven rename or relocation for which the open directory descriptor, file-reference identity, presenter, and opaque provider-authority evidence still identify the same directory object, contents, and authority; only its pathname or ancestors changed. Such a move updates the presented URL, rooted-directory bookmark/file reference, matching active- and retained-journal records, and FSEvents subscription while descriptor-relative and validated File Provider operations continue. It does not increment structural generation, cancel in-flight access, or force reconciliation solely because the path changed; completed work may publish under its existing fences. NV rediscovers authority after the callback. While authority continuity is inconclusive, provider-derived publication and destructive cleanup remain frozen, but editing continues if rooted filesystem identity and local journal durability remain established. Confirmed continuity retains the session. A confirmed provider authority/account change takes the provisional-successor handoff above: fencing rejects every former-session completion immediately, and successful promotion resets authority-scoped discovery and evidence. The handoff preserves the continuously held directory lock, active journal and claim, and pending local obligations rather than creating another journal. An identity failure without positive deletion evidence remains inconclusive and cannot retarget, publish, or clean up. NV removes its app-initiated whole-directory relocation command.
- Local journal durability is independent of provider-authority discovery. `DISPATCH_VNODE_DELETE`, `DISPATCH_VNODE_REVOKE`, deletion accommodation, or any journal append/outbox-drain/barrier-sync failure enters **journal durability suspension** on the main thread: NV first admits the edit already being delivered to the live model, then stops accepting further model mutations, bypasses the ordinary coalescing delay for all admitted journal records, and asks the durability lane to drain and barrier-synchronize them. Those records remain visibly pending and NV never reports them durable from memory or `write` success. Editing resumes only after the same rooted directory identity is positively revalidated, the journal remains namespace-recoverable, the existing descriptor and claim remain valid, and every admitted record has passed the barrier; provider authority may remain unknown while provider-derived publication and cleanup stay frozen. A permanently revoked or unlinked descriptor cannot resume editing in that session. This is a blocking durability interlock with Retry, **Save Current Collection As…**, opening another collection with an abandonment warning, or quitting with explicit discard confirmation, not an ongoing read-only mode.
- `NotationController` has one authoritative main-thread mutation-admission check with independent reason bits. `journalDurabilitySuspended` is selected and cleared only by the root session under the rule above. `lifecycleQuiescing` is selected by `AppController` before termination, root closure, or a controlled transition begins and is cleared only if that operation cancels before its commit boundary. Either reason blocks the mutation; clearing one never clears the other. Sharing the check does not merge their state machines, retry rules, or UI.
- `accommodatePresentedItemDeletionWithCompletionHandler:` is advance notice, not proof that deletion completed. NV enters journal durability suspension, quiesces new root mutations and publication, calls the completion handler after its in-memory state is safe, and classifies the resulting root asynchronously. A missing path, `ENOENT`, failed or stale bookmark, FSEvents root change, unavailable provider, provider eviction, or `DISPATCH_VNODE_REVOKE` alone remains inconclusive. Neither `st_nlink`, `F_GETPATH`, nor a process/file-descriptor census establishes deletion. A vnode-delete event proves that the opened vnode was unlinked; on confirmed ordinary-local storage that is sufficient for **confirmed root loss**, while provider-managed or unknown storage additionally requires validated evidence that no identity-equivalent logical provider item remains at a reachable location. Otherwise deletion classification remains inconclusive. Existing descriptors held by NV or another process do not negate a confirmed namespace loss.
- Provider-managed deletion classification uses one fail-closed operation-local recipe. Root activation first captures the successful opaque File Provider `(domainIdentifier, itemIdentifier)` pair. After a vnode-delete event, positive file-reference, bookmark, or URL-to-identifier results may prove that the same logical item survives but their failure never proves deletion. NV enumerates registered domains, requires the captured domain to remain present, obtains its `NSFileProviderManager`, and calls `getUserVisibleURLForItemIdentifier:completionHandler:` for the captured item. A returned URL counts as survival only after security-scoped coordinated access proves it is a directory and URL-to-identifier round-trip equality reproduces the captured pair. Equal filesystem identity identifies an ordinary move; a different vnode with the same opaque pair identifies provider rematerialization/replacement and still forbids `confirmedGone`, although it cannot make the old unlinked journal descriptor resumable. A different item at the former path is never adopted. Only `NSFileProviderErrorDomain`/`NSFileProviderErrorNoSuchItem` naming that exact captured item from that exact domain manager corroborates the vnode deletion and selects `confirmedGone`. Missing domains, unavailable managers, nil or mismatched URLs, account/provider errors, timeouts, unsupported APIs, and every other error remain inconclusive. An `unknown` root may be promoted later when authority discovery positively establishes ordinary-local storage or this provider recipe succeeds; until then Retry remains available. Provider-backed `confirmedGone` is disabled unless the complete reverse-lookup recipe passes AV-08 on the supported OS/provider matrix.
- Confirmed root loss cancels all old-session publication and never attempts the ordinary old-root closing checkpoint, journal cleanup/demotion, or folder-switch transaction: the old namespace can no longer provide those durability semantics. If a different-identity directory now occupies the former location, NV offers **Save Current Collection As…** (recommended) and **Re-open from Disk**. Reopening explicitly abandons the current in-memory collection after warning, closes the old session, and runs normal startup against the replacement as a fresh root; contents that merely match do not receive special treatment. If no replacement exists, reopening is unavailable. A confirmed-loss prompt offers neither Cancel nor Retry; quitting without saving requires explicit confirmation that the current in-memory collection will be discarded. Inconclusive disappearance retains Retry, **Save Current Collection As…**, opening another collection with an abandonment warning, and quit with explicit discard confirmation because the root may be moved, offline, unmounted, or temporarily unavailable.
- **Save Current Collection As…** freezes the authoritative in-memory store and requires a destination directory exclusively created by NV at a user-selected new name; it never writes into, adopts, scans, or merges an existing directory. While the inaccessible old session remains open, NV creates an unattached destination session and installs through the canonical checkpoint transaction one complete `FrozenNotation` bootstrap whose storage format is single-database and whose notes, user-visible settings, and metadata equal the frozen live store except for the necessarily staged target storage and credential identities. If separate-file storage is intended, NV decodes that verified bootstrap into detached target state, leaving the authoritative source controller unchanged, and reuses the controlled storage-transition export there: atomically create every note file through the existing all-note writer and install a second, final checkpoint that records the intended separate-file format and resulting file metadata. Each checkpoint has a fresh ID and is exactly verified. No destination journal is created and no editing resumes until the final accepted checkpoint is ready; if the process crashes or export fails after bootstrap installation, the destination remains a complete single-database collection and ordinary reopening can retry conversion without a ledger. After final verification NV completes the ordinary post-checkpoint startup path for the destination with a fresh journal, updates the saved bookmark only when the current launch is a permanent collection switch, publishes that controller, and thereby closes the inaccessible old session. It does not delete, demote, transplant, or claim target-checkpoint coverage for any old-root artifact; the old current-host claim remains conservative interrupted-session evidence.
- A Save-As destination is an independent credential owner. Its detached target preferences never archive the source collection's `keychainDatabaseIdentifier`. If the frozen source uses remembered encryption, NV reuses the credential-cutover provisioning leaf before installing the bootstrap: obtain the currently validated passphrase or authenticate again, generate a fresh never-reused target identifier, add the target item without updating another item, read it back, and validate it against the detached target KDF/verifier. Only then may the bootstrap name that identifier. An unremembered or unencrypted target clears the identifier and creates no item. Save As never deletes or updates the source credential. A proven failure before checkpoint installation may best-effort delete the exact newly provisioned target item; an ambiguous installation retains it because a destination checkpoint may name it. A later passphrase cutover may therefore delete only that destination's unique former item without stranding another collection.
- Runtime folder switching applies only while the old root remains writable enough to complete its normal closure. It first freezes mutations to the old root and performs the same root-closing durability transaction as quit: synchronize the journal, settle separate-file obligations, run the final provider barrier, and, when required, install and verify at most one checkpoint. A provably empty active journal takes its independent durable-removal exception; otherwise a verified checkpoint either qualifies cleanup of its covered journals or demotes the remaining closed active journal to retained same-host ownership before switching. Lack of upload qualification does not leave a verified checkpoint attached to a nonempty active journal. A failure before checkpoint verification offers retry or cancel. If the journal itself is durable, an explicit **Switch Anyway** may instead preserve that interrupted old session's journal and active claim because no covering checkpoint exists. Only after one of those conservative outcomes does NV deactivate the old root session, drain and unregister its presenter, release the rooted directory, and begin startup for the new root. Confirmed root loss takes the exceptional policy above instead.
- `NSUbiquityIdentityDidChangeNotification` makes provider authority continuity inconclusive but does not itself prove that the selected root changed authority or that local journal durability failed. NV freezes provider-derived publication and cleanup, preserves the active journal and root lock, marks authority unknown, invalidates authority-scoped current-known and transient resolution evidence, and starts asynchronous authority/capability discovery and reconciliation. Editing may continue while rooted identity and journal durability remain established. Confirmed continuity retains the session; a confirmed authority change takes the fresh-incarnation handoff above. NV neither persists nor logs the identity token. Working offline remains available only under the mandatory-recovery-input rule above.

`ROOT-01` is the normative root-state/action matrix. Root continuity, journal durability, and lifecycle quiescing remain independent reasons, and clearing one never clears another. Safety permissions compose conjunctively: edits are admitted only when every applicable row permits them, and any applicable freeze forbids provider publication or destructive cleanup. User presentation is selected deterministically rather than by an undefined “most restrictive” ordering: confirmed root loss takes precedence; otherwise an already active lifecycle transaction follows its `CKPT-01` caller row; otherwise journal durability suspension uses its blocking preservation/abandonment choices; otherwise authority uncertainty is nonblocking; otherwise operation is normal. A journal failure before a lifecycle transaction's commit aborts that attempt to the durability-suspension presentation. After a verified commit, the monotonic caller completes its specified cleanup/retention path without reopening edits.

| Root state | Admit edits | Provider publication | Destructive cleanup | User actions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active, identity confirmed, journal durable | Yes | Under the ordinary transaction fences | Only through `CLEAN-01` or the empty-journal exception | Normal operation |
| Provider authority/continuity inconclusive, rooted identity and journal durability confirmed | Yes | Frozen until `AUTH-01` is re-established | Frozen | Nonblocking status; automatic retry; ordinary Save As remains available |
| Journal durability suspended, with root continuity confirmed or inconclusive | No | Frozen | Frozen | Retry, **Save Current Collection As…**, open another collection after abandonment warning, or quit after explicit discard confirmation |
| Lifecycle quiescing for quit, switch, or controlled transition | No | Only the already admitted monotonic operation may advance | Only that operation's verified cleanup phase | Retry/cancel before commit where specified, or the caller-specific explicit override |
| Confirmed root loss / old session canceled | No | Never through the old session | Never against the old root | **Save Current Collection As…**; **Re-open from Disk** only when a replacement exists; quit after explicit discard; no Cancel/Retry |

Canceling the **Save Current Collection As…** panel before destination creation returns to the applicable durability-suspension or confirmed-loss prompt and leaves the old store, session, journal descriptor, claims, and credential untouched. Cancellation or failure after exclusive target creation never reattaches that target or closes the old session: before bootstrap verification NV may remove only provably owned empty target residue and its proven unreferenced credential; after bootstrap verification it retains the detached complete destination for ordinary reopening or conversion retry and tells the user where the canceled save remains. No cancellation outcome silently opens the replacement, abandons the live store, or converts timeout into root-loss proof.

## Checkpoint and root-closing transaction

- Termination enters a quiescing state: it rejects new user edits and new synchronization work, lets already admitted sync sessions and external-editing work finish mutating the model, and then freezes the final model state.
- The provider lane then reaches one final barrier. It drains queued presenter work, performs the root session's applicable exact or current-visible reconciliation until that scope's catalog/version-or-current-state snapshots, model generations, and dirty obligation are stable, settles resulting note-file writes, and atomically marks the root session closing so no later callback can publish into this transaction. NV then unregisters and drains the presenter. External changes genuinely arriving afterward remain in the separate files for next-launch reconciliation.
- Every failure before checkpoint installation—including catalog or version enumeration, required materialization, reconciliation, note-file settlement, journal synchronization, or the provider barrier—uses one outcome. Retry keeps waiting. **Quit Anyway** leaves the previous checkpoint, active journal, and host-local active record intact and installs no new checkpoint representing unsettled obligations. `applicationWillTerminate:` performs no fallback flush.
- After all prerequisites settle, NV removes a provably empty active journal through the exception above. If no checkpoint-scoped note, setting, metadata, or tombstone state changed and no retained backlog requires a qualification retry, root closure then installs no checkpoint. Otherwise NV serializes the complete live store once with a fresh random `checkpointID`, atomically installs `Notes & Settings`, synchronizes the directory, and coordinates an exact read-back; an already removed empty journal is absent from that receipt's coverage. Every installation attempt at the semantic boundaries—initial collection creation, startup recovery, bootstrap rebuild, root closure, either half of a storage transition, and the bootstrap and final target installations of **Save Current Collection As…**—routes through one canonical checkpoint transaction and consumes a fresh, never-reused ID; a failed attempt does not make its ID reusable. Installation is verified only when the read-back bytes exactly equal the serialized frozen bytes, those bytes decode successfully, and they contain the expected `checkpointID`; the existing rough structural verifier alone cannot authorize journal deletion or provider-version resolution. `CheckpointIDsAreNeverReused` is checked independently by [QuitSafety.tla](tla/QuitSafety.tla), [StartupSafety.tla](tla/StartupSafety.tla), [ReconciliationSafety.tla](tla/ReconciliationSafety.tla), and [StorageTransitionSafety.tla](tla/StorageTransitionSafety.tla).
- The checkpoint transaction's expected-current evidence is a mandatory local compare-and-swap precondition, never advisory. For ordinary root closure and controlled transition it is the accepted predecessor checkpoint's exact bytes and, when available, the local `NSFileVersion.persistentIdentifier` captured when those bytes were accepted; a newer checkpoint delivered during the session is not an authorized predecessor. Startup recovery names the exact current artifact it classified for replacement. Bootstrap rebuild names either its exact classified predecessor or expected absence when checkpoint absence was authoritatively classified; the second Save-As installation names its verified bootstrap predecessor, and every creation path also carries expected absence. Inside the coordinated replacement accessor and immediately before namespace mutation, NV reopens the leaf relative to the rooted directory and requires that evidence to match exactly. Absence, appearance, byte change, or available-version mismatch performs no replacement and returns the typed pre-install result `NVCheckpointInstallCurrentChanged`. The caller follows `CKPT-01`; no caller silently retries against newly observed evidence or treats another checkpoint as merged settings. A remote change not yet delivered locally remains outside this local CAS: exact fail-on-conflict upload detects it when available, and otherwise lack of exact qualification retains the covered journal and provider versions.

`CKPT-01` is the normative checkpoint-caller outcome matrix. Every row uses the same mechanical `NVCheckpointTransaction`; the matrix defines what its caller may do. `CurrentChanged` always means that the transaction proved no checkpoint namespace mutation occurred. An ambiguous possible-install result never authorizes publication, credential cleanup, journal cleanup, or provider-version resolution and preserves every artifact that could still be referenced.

| Caller | Expected-current evidence | `CurrentChanged` or proven no-install failure | Ambiguous possible-install failure | Cancel/override boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial collection creation | Expected absence | Reclassify the appeared checkpoint/root; never overwrite it as empty | Publish no store; preserve the candidate and return to startup recovery | User may choose another folder or quit; no overwrite override |
| Startup journal recovery | Exact accepted checkpoint being recovered over | Restart discovery/recovery from unchanged artifacts | Preserve every journal/claim and both checkpoint candidates; no editable store | Retry, choose another folder, or quit |
| Bootstrap rebuild | Expected absence, or the exact artifact explicitly classified for replacement | Discard the detached rebuild and restart checkpoint/root discovery; appearance, disappearance, or mismatch is never adopted inside the same attempt | Preserve the detached failure evidence and original artifacts; no partial store | Retry/cancel to startup choices |
| Quit root closure | Accepted predecessor bytes plus available local-version identity | Retry, or **Quit Anyway** with old checkpoint and active claim intact | Retain active journal/claim and all checkpoint candidates; override makes no cleanup claim | Retry or **Quit Anyway** |
| Ordinary folder switch | Same as quit for the old root | Retry/cancel; **Switch Anyway** is allowed only under the pre-checkpoint durable-journal rule | Keep the old root selected unless the user explicitly takes the pre-checkpoint abandonment path | Retry, Cancel, or eligible **Switch Anyway** |
| Controlled storage/credential transition | Accepted predecessor for each old/target boundary | Abort or retry without adopting newly observed settings | Keep accepted settings, credential, journal, and every possible target credential/checkpoint reference | Retry/cancel before verified target commit; no overwrite override |
| Save-As bootstrap | Expected absence in the exclusively created destination | Treat appearance as a destination collision and choose a different new name | Keep source untouched; retain possible target checkpoint and target credential | Cancel returns to `ROOT-01`; never merge/overwrite |
| Save-As final separate-format checkpoint | Exact verified bootstrap predecessor | Keep the bootstrap as the complete detached destination and retry conversion | Keep the bootstrap, possible final checkpoint, and target credential; publish neither target controller nor journal | Cancel/failure retains the detached bootstrap and returns to `ROOT-01` |

- Database recovery versions are not newly reconciled during root closure and no provider version is marked resolved merely because NV installed a checkpoint. The immutable receipt freezes the exact routine-reconciliation and explicit-**Recover Notes** version identifiers that were already eligible, remain durably represented, and are covered by the frozen store. After applicable local checkpoint durability and, under provider management, exact upload qualification of that same receipt, NV resolves each covered version independently. Resolution failure is deferred cleanup: the provider version remains discoverable for a later exact transaction and does not invalidate the checkpoint, require a persistent resolution ledger, or block root closure.
- For a confirmed ordinary local directory, successful exact checkpoint verification and local directory durability make every same-host active or retained journal represented by that checkpoint removable under the existing rule.
- For a provider-managed checkpoint on macOS 26 and later, NV prefers an explicit database-before-cleanup barrier. When that item advertises both pause-sync and fail-upload-on-conflict controls, NV attempts to pause it before checkpoint installation, atomically installs the checkpoint in a short coordinated replacement, and explicitly uploads the stable local version with fail-on-conflict policy. Advertisement authorizes the attempt; only the actual operation results establish success. NV gives that request a transaction-local identity and accepts its callback only when it belongs to the still-pending request and its returned `NSFileVersion` identifies the exact installed and read-back-verified local version containing the same `checkpointID`; callback arrival alone or a matching checkpoint field in a different local version is insufficient. NV then resumes synchronization with preserve-local-changes behavior. Only after both exact-version upload and resume succeed may NV resolve provider versions in that receipt's resolution coverage and coordinate deletion of same-host journals in its journal coverage. `ExplicitUploadCallback` and `ExplicitConfirmationMatchedRequest` in [QuitSafety.tla](tla/QuitSafety.tla) formalize this correlation. The coordinator accessor never remains open during the network upload.
- Unsupported controls or a pause failure before the item becomes paused are not quit failures. NV installs the checkpoint through the ordinary coordinated path and leaves subsequent transfer to the provider. A successful pause is followed by a resume attempt on every upload, conflict, cancellation, and error path. Upload failure, conflict, returned-version mismatch, or resume failure likewise falls back to ordinary synchronization when synchronization is running; none prevents explicit quit override. If the checkpoint was installed and exactly verified locally, orderly termination converts the active claim to a retained claim; failure before that checkpoint leaves the active claim unchanged. If resume itself fails, the checkpoint may remain paused, and startup repairs it using the paused-state resource key before freshness evaluation. A later successfully qualified full checkpoint may clean up the entire same-host retained backlog it covers.
- The explicit upload callback establishes the required remote ordering without claiming a cross-file cloud transaction: NV issues neither covered provider-version resolution nor journal deletion until the provider has accepted the installed checkpoint. In ordinary-upload fallback no such ordering is claimed, so provider versions remain unresolved and an unconfirmed journal remains recovery evidence while the checkpoint and journal synchronize normally. Current-visible reconciliation fallback is orthogonal: it cannot qualify cleanup, but it does not invalidate exact upload qualification obtained independently for the checkpoint.
- On macOS 11.5 through 15, NV uses the strongest available compatibility qualification: after installing the checkpoint it must observe a new not-uploaded or uploading state followed by uploaded, with no intervening local checkpoint write, and then coordinate an exact read-back of the installed `checkpointID` and captured local version. This is not documented version-bound server proof. If the complete post-install transition is missed or any observation is ambiguous, status remains unknown and NV retains the covered journals. Each later quit checkpoint supplies a new transition opportunity and one success retires the complete covered backlog. The macOS 26 version-returning contract is normative for long-term behavior.
- Provider availability and upload status reuse `SyncSessionController`'s `waitForUncommitedChangesWithInvocation:` progress, error, and explicit quit-override behavior. Pending upload waits; override, unknown status, or a surfaced error permits quitting after a locally covered active journal has been transitioned to retained ownership, while every pre-checkpoint failure leaves its active claim intact.
- Termination advances monotonically through one attempt. Reentrant callbacks resume the recorded phase and never repeat reconciliation, install a second checkpoint, or perform a later checkpoint rewrite.
- Local file-first ordering is a crash-consistency rule, not a promise about a provider's cross-file delivery order. NV waits for local separate-file settlement, not upload of every note file; the uploaded complete checkpoint remains recovery evidence if a note-file upload is delayed and is sufficient backstop for resolving only the provider versions named by its exact resolution coverage.

## Provider fallback and user presentation

- Runtime storage authority is provider-managed, confirmed ordinary local storage, or unknown; provider brand is not a correctness state. Authority discovery first queries the resolved existing root itself. For a prospective not-yet-created child or an existing child not yet assigned usable item evidence, it then queries each existing directory ancestor on the same volume, nearest first, and stops at the first positive ubiquitous-item or File Provider URL-domain result or at the volume root. It never crosses a volume boundary, parses a path component, or treats a parent display name as evidence. Any missing/inaccessible ancestor, changed identity during the walk, unsupported API, permission failure, transient provider error, or otherwise inconclusive result leaves authority unknown unless an independent positive provider result already succeeded. Exhausting the walk without positive provider evidence does not by itself prove ordinary-local storage. Opaque item/domain identifiers are equality tokens for continuity only. Confirmed ordinary local storage additionally requires the separately validated positive local-filesystem/exclusion recipe in AV-09; until that recipe is established for a case, the case remains unknown and receives no ordinary-local cleanup or recovery shortcut.
- `WAIT-01`: Provider operations have no correctness timeout and no automatic elapsed-time failure. Startup materialization, rebuild/recovery, final root-closing reconciliation, checkpoint transfer, and Save-As export show app-owned indeterminate progress unless a validated exact-item progress value exists. A UX delay may reveal explanatory text or the already permitted actions, but it never changes evidence. Cancel asks NV to cancel or abandon only its current operation, waits for accessor/completion ownership to return, and then takes the caller's row in `ROOT-01` or `CKPT-01`; it never declares freshness, absence, completeness, root loss, upload, or failed installation. A final close may wait indefinitely while the user leaves it running, but always retains Retry plus Cancel termination/switch before commit, or the caller-specific explicit override after a surfaced failure. Provider work and progress never block the main thread.
- `CAP-01`: Capability checks are operation-scoped. NV checks OS availability, reads an explicit per-item capability value when Apple provides one, then treats the actual asynchronous operation result as authoritative. An advertised bit or previous success only authorizes an attempt. For historical/nonlocal version enumeration, the only stable unsupported classifier is `NSCocoaErrorDomain`/`NSFeatureUnsupportedError` returned by the required `+[NSFileVersion getNonlocalVersionsOfItemAtURL:completionHandler:]` call for an authoritative candidate under the still-active root authority. One such actual result is sufficient to make current-known completeness unavailable for that root session and select current-visible fallback; repeating the same call cannot strengthen that evidence. This promotion says only that exact all-candidate discovery cannot complete—it does not assert that every URL or unrelated provider operation is unsupported. The session suppresses retries caused solely by that missing facility, makes no later exact-completeness claim, and displays the limitation continuously until the session closes. Transient, conflict, cancellation, nil/error ambiguity, a different domain/code, or a result from another URL/session takes the ordinary incomplete/dirty path. A fresh root session attempts exact discovery again; NV persists neither the result, a provider capability profile, nor a provider-brand-derived mode bit.
- Reconciliation capability and checkpoint upload qualification are independent. Current-visible fallback never qualifies upload or journal cleanup itself, but a separately matched exact checkpoint upload may resolve the receipt's already eligible provider versions and clean its covered journals. Conversely, exact version enumeration never proves that a checkpoint uploaded.
- First-class behavior is derived at runtime from provider-managed authority, `PlainTextFormat`, disabled encryption, and the evidence required by the current operation. iCloud Drive is the validated and supported configuration. Dropbox and other File Providers receive off-main coordinated access, compare-and-swap writes, transactional scans, generic retry/error reporting, and any additional standard behavior they successfully provide, but NV does not promise it.
- Unknown provider authority retains the journal and uses coordinated access without claiming provider or local-only durability. Only confirmed ordinary local storage uses the ordinary local-durability cleanup rule; provider-managed cleanup requires the applicable exact upload qualification.
- `-[NSFileManager ubiquityIdentityToken]` and its change notification describe iCloud account availability, not ownership of an arbitrary selected root. Container/domain display names are UI-only. NV never parses paths, raw domain identifiers, private extended attributes, or display names to infer provider type.
- A current startup checkpoint opens without synchronization UI. Required materialization shows progress. Working offline remains visible until exact reconciliation succeeds or stable current-visible fallback settles visible work; the latter leaves a session-continuous nonblocking “file-version history unavailable” status instead of an activity spinner or a claim of full synchronization. Ordinary background reconciliation reuses the existing synchronizing indicator.
- Successful text merges are silent. Preserved alternatives become ordinary conflict notes and produce one coalesced notification. Database and unclaimed-journal recovery candidates remain discoverable without interrupting normal launches.
- Upload pending/unknown means the checkpoint is durable on this Mac but exact-version upload confirmation is absent. NV does not separately report “saved in journal” and “saved to note file.”
- Root-loss presentation is independent of cloud branding. Confirmed deletion suppresses Cancel/Retry and offers only the preservation/opening actions that remain possible; inconclusive availability keeps retry visible and never claims that the root was deleted. Authority uncertainty alone remains nonblocking while local journal durability is established, whereas journal durability suspension blocks further editing and exposes the preservation, abandonment, and quit choices above.

## Migration, security, and compatibility

- Compatibility obligations are determined only by artifacts and behavior represented by commits reachable from `origin/master`. This specification describes one unreleased epoch-5 target; superseded epoch-5 drafts and local implementations absent from `origin/master` require no transitional reader, migration, alias, dual-write, or behavioral compatibility. The epoch-4 compatibility contract remains explicit below.
- A passphrase, encryption, or storage-format change is a controlled-restart **credential cutover**, never a mutation of the accepted live `NotationPrefs` or its Keychain item. NV constructs an isolated target preferences snapshot while the accepted checkpoint and old generic-password item remain unchanged. If the target is encrypted and the user elects to remember its passphrase, NV generates a fresh never-reused `keychainDatabaseIdentifier`, adds a new item without updating any existing account, reads its bytes back, and validates them against the target snapshot's KDF/verifier before checkpoint installation. A collision burns that identifier and retries with another. If the target does not use a remembered passphrase, its staged identifier is cleared and no target item is required.
- The target checkpoint archives that isolated preferences snapshot and, when applicable, its fresh Keychain identifier. Exact installation and verification through the canonical checkpoint transaction are the cutover commit. Only afterward does an orderly transition attempt to delete the old Keychain item and then restart to publish the target preferences. Old-item deletion is best-effort and never rolls back or prevents restart from a verified target; failure is reported as a nonfatal credential-cleanup warning. A crash before target installation leaves the old checkpoint and old item usable and may leave at most one unreferenced target item from that interrupted attempt; a crash after installation leaves the target checkpoint referring to its already verified target item and may leave the old item as inert residue. Retry creates another fresh target identifier when necessary and uses no migration ledger. NV never automatically selects or deletes an unreferenced item merely because it shares NV's Keychain service name.
- Entering the first-class provider-managed storage contract explicitly warns that notes will be stored in clear text. If the source is RTF or HTML, the same confirmation also states that conversion preserves plaintext content but discards rich-text/HTML formatting and attachments. NV extends the existing storage-format conversion confirmation flow with this new formatting/attachment-loss disclosure and requires affirmative confirmation before proceeding. The transition uses one controlled restart transaction because disabling encryption changes both the checkpoint and journal keys. NV freezes editing; settles separate-file and journal obligations; installs and verifies a complete old-format, old-key checkpoint; retires the old journal only after its applicable durability barrier; exports every live note through the plaintext writer; verifies that the provider catalog token is unchanged across the export fence; installs the complete unencrypted checkpoint; and restarts with a freshly created journal before accepting edits. The export fence is catalog-only and does not require historical/nonlocal version enumeration. The storage transition is complete only after every phase succeeds. Until the final checkpoint is installed, its old format is the restart marker: cancel or failure leaves that complete checkpoint recoverable, while a retry loads it, reruns plaintext export idempotently, and preserves any divergent file as external work. No migration-phase ledger or compatibility with an intermediate epoch-5 draft is required.
- If a selected folder is provider-managed, or authority rediscovery detects that Finder moved the active folder under provider management, an incompatible format or enabled encryption becomes the same blocking controlled transition. NV keeps admitted edits journal-durable and offers convert and restart, choose another folder, or quit; it neither silently changes format, records a contradictory mode flag, downgrades to best-effort operation, nor runs a read-only mode.
- A legacy checkpoint remains readable and receives a random `checkpointID` only on the next verified modern save. Until then, a new modern journal uses the appendix's tag-`0x02` SHA-256 identity derived from the exact loaded bytes so startup need not rewrite the checkpoint before presenting the UI. Migration never regenerates an existing note UUID. When legacy and modern journals coexist, the legacy exact-name journal is recovered and checkpointed first; only then is the modern journal evaluated against that replacement checkpoint.
- Concurrent epoch-4 and modern editing is unsupported. If a legacy exact-name WAL appears while modern NV is active, NV stops new filesystem mutations, preserves its journal, and asks the user to quit both processes and relaunch modern NV.
- Checkpoint and journal IDs are fresh, never-reused random opaque values; no ordering or ancestry is inferred from them. A synthesized recovery note alone uses the deterministic identity described above. NV records no hostname, hardware serial, iCloud account identifier, or other stable machine identity in shared files.
- Existing checksums—and encryption where used outside the first-class provider-managed storage contract—detect accidental corruption and malformed data; they do not authenticate files against a malicious writer. Invalid or mismatched artifacts never authorize append, replay, resolution, or deletion.
- Diagnostics contain no note text, filenames, tags, encryption material, account identifiers, or path-bearing error descriptions. Storage logs are limited to operation, count, error domain, and error code; no telemetry is added.

## Epoch-5 persistence-format appendix

These layouts are normative compatibility contracts. All multibyte integers are unsigned big-endian, UUID bytes use RFC 4122 network order, and no C-structure padding is serialized. Unknown tags, unsupported schema/header versions, malformed lengths or types, and checksum failures never authorize append, replay, claim ownership, recovery cleanup, or provider-version resolution.

### Checkpoint and base identities

- A modern `FrozenNotation` keyed archive stores `checkpointID` as `NSData` containing exactly 16 UUID bytes. An epoch-4 archive has no such key.
- The canonical checkpoint-base identity used outside the checkpoint archive is one tag byte followed by its fixed payload: tag `0x01` plus the same 16 modern UUID bytes (17 bytes total), or tag `0x02` plus the SHA-256 digest of the exact unchanged epoch-4 checkpoint bytes (33 bytes total). There is no length prefix inside this value and no string form on disk.
- Writers emit UUID text only for filenames and preference keys as uppercase ASCII `8-4-4-4-12` hexadecimal with hyphens. Readers accept either hexadecimal case in that exact hyphenated shape, normalize to 16 bytes, and treat two names or claims that decode to the same UUID as ambiguous duplicates rather than separate journals.

Identity vectors:

| Meaning | Canonical bytes (hex) |
| --- | --- |
| Modern `00112233-4455-6677-8899-AABBCCDDEEFF` | `0100112233445566778899aabbccddeeff` |
| Epoch-4 SHA-256 bytes `00` through `1f` | `02000102030405060708090a0b0c0d0e0f101112131415161718191a1b1c1d1e1f` |

### Modern journal filename and header version 1

The shared filename is `Interim Note-Changes.<journalID>`; for journal `10213243-5465-7687-98A9-BACBDCEDFE0F` the exact emitted name is `Interim Note-Changes.10213243-5465-7687-98A9-BACBDCEDFE0F`.

| 8 | 2 | Header-format version, `0x0001` |
| 10 | 2 | Total header length, including the trailing CRC |
| 12 | 16 | Journal UUID bytes |
| 28 | 2 | Checkpoint-base-identity byte length: `0x0011` or `0x0021` |
| 30 | 17 or 33 | Canonical checkpoint-base identity above |
| 47 or 63 | 4 | zlib CRC-32, big-endian, over every preceding header byte from offset 0 through the final identity byte |

### Directory lock and current-host journal claims

- The permanent lock leaf is exactly `.nv-root.lock`. It is exclusively created as a regular file with mode `0600` when absent, opened without following symlinks, and held with a nonblocking exclusive advisory lock for the root-session lifetime. Its canonical contents are zero bytes; readers ignore content and never truncate an existing regular lock file. It is never removed during normal close and is excluded from reconciliation by exact name (as well as by the ordinary hidden-file rule).
- Direct current-host keys use `NVActiveJournal.<journalID>` and `NVRetainedJournal.<journalID>` with the canonical uppercase UUID suffix above. An active value is the rooted-directory bookmark as a direct `NSData` property-list value—there is no wrapper dictionary. A retained value is a property-list dictionary with exactly these required version-1 entries: `schemaVersion` = integer `1`, `rootBookmark` = bookmark `NSData`, and `coveringCheckpointID` = exactly 16 UUID bytes in `NSData`. A missing key, wrong type/length, unsupported schema version, key/header UUID mismatch, or ambiguous duplicate preserves the preference and artifact but grants no append, recovery, or cleanup authority.
- Foundation's binary-plist bytes are not a compatibility vector; the key names, value classes, lengths, and semantic values above are the contract. `CFPreferencesSynchronize` ordering remains the durability contract.

### Provider-version ancestry archive field

`NoteObject` and `DeletedNoteObject` use the optional keyed-archive key `providerVersionRepresentationsV1`. Its value is an `NSArray` of `NSDictionary` objects. Each dictionary has `versionIdentifier`, the opaque object returned by `NSFileVersion.persistentIdentifier` and archived directly, and `role`, an `NSNumber` integer: `1` candidate/original, `2` merge input, `3` local-win ancestry, `4` conflict preservation, or `5` identity-bound tombstone. Pairs are deduplicated by `isEqual:` identifier equality plus role; array order and keyed-archive bytes have no meaning. A malformed or unknown entry is ignored for resolution eligibility while the core note or tombstone remains readable. Epoch 4 ignores the whole optional field. AV-05's persistent-identifier archive round trip and epoch-4 compatibility fixtures validate this semantic shape rather than requiring byte-identical `NSKeyedArchiver` output across OS releases.

## Formal-model assumptions and implementation obligations

- The TLA+ modules treat atomic replacement and directory synchronization, immutable persistent version identifiers and immutable coordinated version snapshots, the truth of any implementation assertion that every authoritative note candidate and its exact version set were discovered, correct checksum and decoder results, advisory-lock behavior, coordinator accessor semantics, and positive confirmed-root-loss classification as environmental assumptions. Their `Versions` excludes the independent fixed checkpoint/journal/lock and hidden `.nv-write-<UUID>` projection; visible conflict-recovery note files remain in `Versions`. Artifact status cannot enter the note decision kernel or satisfy/block its dirty obligation. If the implementation cannot establish one of those assumptions for an operation, it classifies the note snapshot as incomplete or the status as unknown, preserves existing artifacts and versions, retains the applicable journal and dirty obligation, and does not publish destructive changes, resolve evidence, or claim completion. The sole narrower publication scope follows `CAP-01`: [CurrentVisibleFallbackSafety.tla](tla/CurrentVisibleFallbackSafety.tla) models routine reconciliation's separately fenced commit, operational dirty consumption, unchanged preexisting resolution eligibility, and root-session reset boundary, while [StartupSafety.tla](tla/StartupSafety.tla) models all-or-nothing current-visible bootstrap plus session-continuous limited-history presentation. The assumption boundary is summarized in the [TLA+ model README](tla/README.md), the Apple behaviors requiring real-system confirmation are enumerated in [Apple API Contract Validation](Apple-API-Contract-Validation.md), and the first-class fail-closed publication boundary is expressed by `CompleteFence` in [ReconciliationInterface.tla](tla/ReconciliationInterface.tla).
- [JournalLifecycle.tla](tla/JournalLifecycle.tla) is deliberately a one-acting-Mac refinement: its active and retained claim sets denote only that Mac's `kCFPreferencesCurrentHost` domain. [HostLocalJournalOwnership.tla](tla/HostLocalJournalOwnership.tla) supplies the separate two-host refinement, with shared journal/checkpoint artifacts but host-indexed preferences and locks; no claim, receipt, or upload qualification is copied between hosts.
- Ambiguous-claim recovery deliberately composes existing abstractions rather than adding another Cartesian-product model: [JournalLifecycle.tla](tla/JournalLifecycle.tla) proves that ambiguity authorizes no automatic action, while [MultiJournalRecoverySafety.tla](tla/MultiJournalRecoverySafety.tla) checks all-candidate replay and one covering-checkpoint cleanup semantics. Exact candidate snapshotting, UI entry, cancellation before irreversible transition, completed-member persistence, partial-failure fresh-journal suppression, and the controller route between those obligations remain integration tests. [StartupSafety.tla](tla/StartupSafety.tla) abstracts a complete rebuilt note set and now models absent-checkpoint expected-evidence installation; concrete extension classification, per-file decoding, and detached conversion remain bootstrap implementation tests.
- [CrossMachineDurability.tla](tla/CrossMachineDurability.tla) is the bounded distributed glue refinement: it follows one acknowledged edit through actual journal, checkpoint, and note contents; separate provider authority and per-Mac replicas; independently reordered checkpoint, journal-create, journal-delete, and note deliveries; exact provider checkpoint coverage before journal deletion; and host-local preference transitions. A Mac that observes a replica omitting the edit remains noneditable or dirty while recoverable evidence exists. The ordinary configuration makes no provider-progress assumption; the separate `CrossMachineConvergence.cfg` puts fair settled-note upload and reader delivery directly into the specification and checks direct eventual-replica and eventual-representation properties. [CrossMachineGenerationSafety.tla](tla/CrossMachineGenerationSafety.tla) supplies the deliberately separate cardinality refinement: two distinct edits in two journal generations, retained-coverage advancement or crash rollback at four persistence boundaries, mandatory equality-only mismatch recovery, checkpoint-3 backlog qualification, reordered deletion with reader-visible omission, and writer-machine loss from every post-install phase. Physical loss of work that never left that machine is tracked explicitly outside the distributed durability guarantee. Keeping these dimensions separate avoids multiplying the larger model's unrelated startup and preference states.
- [StorageTransitionSafety.tla](tla/StorageTransitionSafety.tla) models Keychain bindings as host-local artifacts and explores copy-on-write target provisioning, exact checkpoint cutover, crash residue, and post-commit old-item removal. Encrypted Save As reuses its fresh add/read-back-before-install precondition but never its source-item deletion action. Because the model contains one collection, independent credential ownership across multiple Save-As destinations remains an [AV-14](Apple-API-Contract-Validation.md#av-14--copy-on-write-keychain-credential-cutover) implementation and real-system obligation. The model assumes that Security-framework add, read-back, and delete results are reported truthfully and assumes no transaction with checkpoint replacement.
- The TLA+ modules assume truthful local journal-durability outcomes and do not model the concrete AppKit reason mask. Selecting its lifecycle reason refines the modeled quiescing/transition phase; selecting its durability reason after vnode deletion or revoke, deletion accommodation, or append/outbox/barrier failure removes behavior rather than authorizing a new publication, cleanup, or liveness claim. `StartupSafety` explicitly models expected-absence/exact-predecessor rebuild installation, rejection after an absent checkpoint appears, and rejection when an unacceptable predecessor is replaced by another artifact with the same status; other callers' expected-current CAS remains the safety-strengthening refinement that removes an install action when its environmental precondition is false. Neither boundary weakens a modeled safety condition or adds an unsupported liveness promise; AV-08, deterministic WAL fault injection, and checkpoint-race tests establish their concrete behavior.
- Safety configurations apply weak fairness only to work NV controls once it remains enabled. NV does not assume that a provider, network, user, unavailable version, or unreadable artifact eventually succeeds. The separately named `CrossMachineConvergence.cfg` is a counterfactual liveness check that explicitly assumes settled-note upload and delivery; none of its provider fairness is imported into a safety result. “Current-known” therefore remains a claim about one fenced provider snapshot, never proof that no unseen remote work exists.
- Passing a model does not prove the Objective-C implementation, Apple API behavior, real `NVMergedText` output, filesystem identity stability, UUIDv5 or SHA-256 byte vectors, filename collision and Unicode/case behavior, UI disclosure, thread affinity, redacted diagnostics, or epoch-4 archive compatibility. Those remain implementation review, deterministic vector, integration, filesystem-matrix, and crash-point test obligations; the formal model supplies the required safety contract against which those tests are judged.
