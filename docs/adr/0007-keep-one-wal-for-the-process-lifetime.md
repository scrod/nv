# Keep one active WAL per editable root session

During ordinary operation NV will not periodically checkpoint `Notes & Settings` or rotate its WAL. One active WAL remains the durability record for one editable root-session lifetime; switching roots or completing a startup recovery closes that lifetime and creates a fresh WAL only after the successor checkpoint is accepted. After editing stops during quit or ordinary folder switching, NV synchronizes the active WAL, settles separate-file writes, and installs at most one complete root-closing checkpoint while the old root remains writable enough to accept it. Confirmed root loss takes the separate root-loss policy and never treats a checkpoint in a newly saved collection as covering the inaccessible old WAL. On the ordinary path NV removes a nonempty WAL after ordinary local durability, or after exact-checkpoint upload confirmation for a provider-managed root. When provider qualification is unavailable or ambiguous, NV closes a nonempty WAL and preserves same-host ownership as a retained-journal claim tied to the locally verified covering checkpoint instead of making it unclaimed. A provably header-only WAL is removed durably without upload qualification; when no checkpoint-scoped state changed and no retained backlog needs a retry checkpoint, closure also skips checkpoint installation.

Recovery validates records sequentially and may consume only the complete valid prefix; a torn final record or corrupt interior stops replay and remains preserved. Exact-base journals replay normally, already-represented journals require only durable cleanup, and base-mismatched journals may contribute deterministic nondeletion conflict notes but never overwrite, delete, or restore settings. LSNs establish order only within one journal. Retained covering identities use equality only, so an older unadvanced claim is preserved but must take the ordinary mismatch recovery path. A later complete checkpoint may freeze any number of already represented same-host journals into its exact coverage; one successful applicable upload qualification then authorizes their independent cleanup. A retained backlog alone makes the next orderly root closure install a fresh-ID cleanup checkpoint so older-system upload qualification can be retried without periodic or typing-time checkpointing. Startup recovery may install a recovered checkpoint before creating a fresh WAL, and storage-format changes use a controlled restart, but NV adds no Bloom filter, sealing, absorption ledger, terminal digest, successor chain, or routine database-version merge.

`MultiJournalRecoverySafety` checks three records across two recovered journals: each journal's internal order is fixed while the other journal may interleave arbitrarily. It covers independent and conflicting nondeletion states, equal-state idempotence in either cross-journal order, within-journal delete-then-update and update-then-delete, cross-journal delete/update preservation, and anti-resurrection. It assigns no order between journals; `JournalRecoverySafety` remains the detailed byte-prefix model and independently exercises its existing already-represented duplicate path.

Normative requirements: `START-01`, `DUR-01`, `CLEAN-01`.
